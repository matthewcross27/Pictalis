# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Casual iPhone users aged 18-35. They've just come back from a trip, a graduation shoot, a concert, or a birthday party and have 100-300 photos they genuinely care about but haven't looked at properly since. They're in a nostalgic, slightly excited headspace — not processing files, revisiting memories. Sessions happen on the couch or commute, not at a desk.

## Product Purpose

Pictalis helps users quickly find the photos they'll actually come back to. It removes the cognitive weight of scanning a long gallery by breaking the decision into the simplest possible unit — "which of these two do I prefer?" — and surfaces a ranked shortlist with minimal comparisons. The job is done when the user has a set of 10-20 favorites they're glad they found.

The name is a portmanteau of "Pic" (photography) and the root of "Talisman" — a small, cherished object of deep sentimental value. The framing is deliberate: this is not file management, it is preservation of keepsakes.

## Brand Personality

Artsy, warm, satisfying. The brand draws thematic and aesthetic inspiration from *Pyralis pictalis*, a species of moth characterized by warm, organic coloration. Just as a moth is drawn to light, the app helps users sort through chaotic digital noise to locate the glowing, standout moments in a collection.

The visual character is photographic and quietly stylized — it stays out of the way during use. Choosing a photo should feel like a small pleasure, not a chore. The energy is closer to a well-designed zine or photobook than a productivity tool.

**Color direction:** Warm, nostalgic tones derived from nature — ochres, dusty ambers, soft creams, and velvety grays. Avoid clinical blues and stark blacks. The palette should feel like an old photograph, not a SaaS dashboard.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS interfaces: cream backgrounds, gradient purple CTAs, the default "modern startup" aesthetic.
- Google Photos / Apple Photos: utilitarian file-manager feel — photos are data to be managed, not memories to be surfaced.
- Tinder-style swiping: gamified comparison mechanics feel cheap and impermanent for photos that matter.
- Heavy in-app branding: the app should not market itself to you while you're using it.

## Design Principles

1. **Photos are always the hero.** Every UI decision defers to the images. The chrome exists to serve the photo, never compete with it.
2. **Each tap should feel satisfying.** Momentum and micro-feedback matter. Choosing should feel decisive and good, not like processing a queue.
3. **Artsy restraint.** Personality lives in proportion, type, and color — not decoration. Inspired by VSCO and Day One: clean, considered, subtly distinctive.
4. **Memories, not metrics.** Language and framing treat these as personal moments. "Comparison 47 of N" is wrong. "Your favorites are almost ready" is right.
5. **Transparent progress, invisible complexity.** Users always know roughly where they are in the session. The Elo math and stage system are never exposed — only their effect (better and better pairs).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard iOS accessibility: Dynamic Type support throughout, basic VoiceOver labels on interactive elements. No custom reduced-motion requirement beyond respecting the system preference.
